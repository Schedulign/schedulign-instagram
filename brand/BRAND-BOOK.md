# Schedulign brand book

The complete description of who Schedulign is, who it serves, what it says and how it sounds. Written to be pasted or uploaded into any AI tool as the brief for content. Source: Sam's answers, September 2026. Where this disagrees with an older document, this wins.

---

## 1. In one paragraph

Schedulign is the booking page that runs your business. It gives independent professionals, starting with solo fitness and wellness pros, one link where clients pick a time, sign the waiver, pay and get reminded, with no account to make. It exists because people who are great at their work lose their evenings to texting clients back and forth, chasing no-shows and sorting paperwork. Schedulign takes that admin off their plate, simply and in a friendly way, so they can get back to the work they love. It is free to start.

## 2. The company

- **What it is:** a booking page for people who sell their time. Clients book, pay and change bookings from one link, without an account, under rules the pro sets for each kind of session.
- **Why it exists:** watching freelancers drown in admin: five messages to book one session, replies at 11pm, no-shows with no policy to point to.
- **Mission:** make booking effortless, for the pro and for their clients.
- **Where it's going:** within three years, the go-to booking page for solo pros in the US.
- **The founder:** stays behind the brand for now. Schedulign and Slot are the face.
- **Plans:** Free (everything in Pro, up to 5 bookings a week, with a small "Powered by Schedulign" line), Pro ($20 a month: unlimited bookings, no badge), Teams (Pro for a team, plus $10 per member seat). Every plan is $0 during early access.

## 3. Who it's for

### The primary customer: the independent fitness and wellness pro

- **Who:** personal trainers, massage therapists, therapists and other wellness pros who see clients by appointment and work for themselves.
- **Scope: appointments, not classes.** Schedulign books appointments (one client, or a small group on one booking). Classes, where many separate people book the same slot, are out of scope, so studio class schedules are not the audience.
- **Stage:** any. Just gone independent, established with a roster of regulars, or doing it on the side.
- **Size:** about 10 to 25 sessions a week.
- **How they book today:** texts and DMs (Instagram, WhatsApp, SMS), one conversation per session.
- **What they're like:** mostly 25 to 45, phone-first (they run the business from their phone), US-based.
- **Top pains:** the endless back-and-forth; no-shows and late cancellations.
- **What finally pushes them to try a booking page:** a bad week. A string of no-shows, a double booking, an evening lost to texts.
- **What they fear about switching:** losing the personal touch with their regulars.
- **What success looks like six months in:** their evenings back, and a fuller calendar.

### Everyone else

Other solo pros are welcome and appear in the content: stylists and barbers, tutors and music teachers, photographers, mobile detailers, cleaners, consultants. Small teams and studios come later, as what solo pros grow into (the Teams plan).

## 4. Competition and positioning

- **The real competitor is staying on DMs.** Most of the audience doesn't use another tool; they text. The message is never "switch from Brand X". It's "stop doing it by hand".
- **Never name another product**, and never claim what another product can or cannot do. Prove Schedulign's side only.
- **Positioning line:** booking a time is the easy part; Schedulign handles the time and everything around it: the place, the price, the paperwork, getting paid, the reminders. Meetings are welcome too; never say or imply "not for meetings".
- **The strongest reason to choose it:** it's **simple and friendly**. Set up in minutes, easy for clients, no bloat.
- **Never look worse than anyone at:** a polished, trustworthy booking page; booking in a few taps with no account; calendar sync (see the truth rules on Google Calendar).

## 5. Messaging

- **Tagline:** "The booking page that runs your business."
- **The one takeaway:** it takes the admin off me.
- **Lead features for fitness and wellness:** waivers signed at booking, and getting paid.
- **The objection to answer:** "My clients just text me, DMs work fine." Show the hidden cost of the back-and-forth, never mock the habit.
- **The fear to answer:** losing the personal touch. The answer: the booking page handles the logistics, so your messages with clients can be about them, not your calendar.
- **Price:** value first, price later. Lead with the problem solved; mention free lightly or not at all. When plans come up: start free, upgrade to Pro when you're busier than 5 bookings a week.
- **Reassurances that are always true:** no card needed to start; cancel anytime.
- **Help promised to new users:** the help center (schedulign.com/help), email support at admin@schedulign.com, personal setup help for early users, and "Report a problem" on every page.
- **Where to send people:** the homepage, schedulign.com (the link in bio).
- **Call to action (captions):** "Hand the admin to your booking page. Link in bio: schedulign.com"

### Four message pillars (every piece proves at least one)

| Pillar | What it means | Proof (all shipped) |
|---|---|---|
| **More than a time slot** | The details around a session are built in. | A place per session (your list, the client's own address, or shared only after booking) plus access notes; a price for every group size and length; peak and discount hours; your own booking questions; waivers signed with a typed e-signature while booking, with the signed name, date and version kept. |
| **Getting paid is part of it** | Money is handled with the booking. | Card at booking through your own Stripe, or the offline ways you already take money (with "Mark paid"); tips; discount codes at checkout; automatic or manual refunds by your policy. Card payments carry only Stripe's fee, nothing added by Schedulign. The Free plan has all of it. |
| **A fuller calendar** | Cancellations and quiet hours get filled; regulars come first. | Reminder emails before each session; day waitlists where a freed time is held two hours for the first in line; openings alerts; clients can ask for times you didn't list; request-only sessions; perks for regulars (book further ahead, book instantly). |
| **Simple for everyone** | Easy for you, effortless for clients. | Clients never make an account; they book in a few taps and change or cancel from their confirmation link, inside the windows you set; the confirmation lands in their calendar; your rules per session; a QR code and a website widget; live in minutes. |

## 6. Voice and personality

- **If Schedulign were a person:** the clever friend. Witty, sharp, a little mischievous about how silly the old way is, always on the freelancer's side.
- **Writing style:** casual and plain. Contractions, short sentences, everyday words. "You're booked."
- **Humor:** relatable "that's me" moments: the 11pm text, the 6am no-show, the waiver pile. The joke is always the situation, never a client.
- **Emojis:** a few, sparingly: one or two in a caption at most, only where they add something.
- **Always:** second person ("you"); clients book **sessions**; clear and specific.
- **Never:** "coach" or "coaching"; "we", "our" or "us" (the brand speaks as Schedulign, or as Slot in first person); exclamation marks; competitor names; health or results claims; body or weight themes; hype words ("revolutionary", "game-changing").

### On-brand examples

- "Five texts to book one session. Or one link."
- "It's 11pm. Your booking page is still working. You don't have to be."
- "6:00am. Nobody came. Write the policy before you need it."
- "The waiver, signed while they book. Not in a pile somewhere."
- "Your regulars still get you. They just stop getting 'what time works for you?'"

### Off-brand examples

- "Revolutionize your scheduling with our AI-powered platform!" (hype, "our", AI, exclamation mark)
- "Better than Calendly." (names a competitor)
- "Get your clients results faster." (results claim)

## 7. Slot, the character

- **Who:** Slot is the booking page, drawn as a lime calendar tile with a navy top band, binder rings, dot eyes and a smile.
- **Personality:** an eager helper. Loves a tidy calendar, visibly happy when something books, always ready.
- **Pronoun:** "it" or simply "Slot". Never "he" or "she".
- **Role:** an occasional narrator. Slot fronts intros and some tips ("Hi, I'm Slot, your booking page.") and appears in scenes whenever the booking page does the work. The freelancers lead the stories.
- **Expressions:** the everyday face (open eyes, smile). The booked face (happy closed eyes, a check mark for a smile) means booked and nothing else.

## 8. Visual identity

- **Logo:** Slot. The tile is the app icon and avatar; the header logo is the lowercase wordmark "schedulign" with Slot standing on the i as its dot; Slot sitting on the h is for banners and merch. Files and rules: `brand/README.md`.
- **Colors:** navy `#303159` (wordmark, text, bands), lime `#b9d32c` (Slot and anything the booking page is doing: a button, a check), ink `#1f2238` (eyes, fine lines). Lime means the booking page is at work; it isn't used for decoration.
- **Type:** the wordmark is Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold. The product uses Bricolage Grotesque for headings and Instrument Sans for text. The illustrations' captions use a rounded, friendly bold (Fredoka).
- **Illustration style ("Lamplight"):** flat, friendly cartoon scenes of freelancers at work. Daylight scenes for daytime moments, warm lamplight for early mornings and late nights. One short line of white text with a dark outline per slide; a small story across a carousel (problem, fix, the client's side, a happy ending).
- **The cast:** Dev (trainer; leads fitness), Maya (stylist), Jonah (mobile detailer, SHINE van), Rosa (tutor), Priya (photographer), plus a wellness regular to come (a massage therapist). The look stays the same from post to post.
- **Formats:** illustrated carousels; short animated videos (Reels, also for TikTok); real product screen recordings on a phone; simple text tip cards; meme formats adapted to freelancer life. Phone screens always show the product's real labels.
- **Never:** stock photos, real customers (for now), before/after body images.

## 9. Truth rules

Everything said must be true today. The help center (schedulign.com/help) is the source: if it doesn't say the product does something, the content doesn't either.

- **Never mention what isn't built.** Not in the product: class passes and packages, memberships, SMS reminders, a client app. Don't mention them, promise them or hint at them.
- **Out of scope:** classes, where many separate people book the same slot. Schedulign books appointments; never position it for class schedules.
- **Cleared (Sep 28 2026):** Google Calendar sync and Google Meet links (Google verified the app), and online card payments (a real host has taken a card). Both can be featured.
- **No future promises:** no launch dates, no "coming soon".
- **No health or results claims.** Schedulign books the time; it promises no outcome.

## 10. Channels and community

- **Instagram:** @schedulign. Five posts a week, weekdays, about 11:20am Pacific. A mix of series: feature spotlights, "Under the hood" explainers, standalone episodes of a recurring character's work (never numbered), before/after posts and tips. Intro posts pinned.
- **Next:** TikTok, with animated versions of the same stories.
- **Replies:** the founder answers comments and DMs by hand.
- **Engagement:** follow and leave genuinely helpful comments on independent trainers' and wellness pros' posts, by hand, never automated.
- **Market:** the US: US English, US dollars, US seasons and holidays.

## 11. Goals and measures

- **Goal:** sign-ups.
- **Measured monthly:** link taps from the bio, new accounts created, and saves and shares per post (which content people find useful or relatable).
